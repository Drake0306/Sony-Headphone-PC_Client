Five design languages for the ULT WEAR app

Each direction is shown twice: the menu-bar popover you'll use a dozen times a day, and the full window you open to customise. Pick one, mix two, or tell me what's missing and I'll draw more. Nothing here is built yet.

What the research fixed before any of these were drawnDark-first, because the number one request on Sony's and Bose's apps is a dark mode. Everything above the fold, because the number one complaint about Sound Connect is a hero image pushing controls off screen. No promotion, no account wall, small footprint, because that is what separates "premium" from "bloatware" in companion software.
Controls match the hardwareULT is a three-position switch (Off, ULT1, ULT2) like the button on the cup. Noise control is a three-way strip (NC, Ambient, Off) with a 20-step ambient level and Focus on Voice that only appear in Ambient. EQ is five bands plus a bigger Clear Bass, which is the device's real DSP, not a free-form curve it cannot honour.
Platform rulesOn macOS 27, glass belongs on chrome (sidebar, toolbar, popover), never on content. Windows 11 reserves Acrylic for flyouts and Mica for windows. Every direction below is drawn so the popover can be a real native popover on Mac and a tray flyout on Windows with the same language.
What none of the sixty existing clients doA device render that reflects state, a pending or confirmed state on every control, an onboarding flow, dB-labelled EQ, battery history. The directions below leave room for those; the one that drew unprompted "looks amazing" praise in the wild was a dot-matrix battery history chart.

Stage

Direction 1 of 5

The ULT campaign is dark, blue-and-violet lit concert imagery. Stage takes that light source and makes it the room the controls sit in. One oversized number carries the screen, the ULT control is the only warm thing on it, and a live sound-pressure meter is the only thing that moves.

Where it shines

  • Most on-brand for a bass-first product; the cool factor is immediate.
  • One moving element (the meter) uses data the device already exposes.

Risks

  • Glows tip into "gamer RGB" fast. The rule is one accent per surface and 1px rules elsewhere.
  • Needs a light theme eventually, and this language resists it.

Platform fit

  • Popover works as a custom NSPanel on Mac and a Tauri or WinUI flyout on Windows; no system material needed.
  • Looks the same on all three platforms, which is a strength for identity and a weakness for nativeness.

Panel

Direction 2 of 5

The headphones are hardware, so the app is a piece of hardware too: an aluminium face plate in a black bezel, one monospace face, a knob for ambient level, faders for EQ, a three-position switch for ULT. Everything is one or two clicks deep and nothing is rounded.

Where it shines

  • Feels like a device, not a settings page. The knob and faders invite playing with sound.
  • Light by default, so it stands apart from every dark-card Sony client.

Risks

  • Reads as a Teenage Engineering homage unless the colourway chips and Sony-specific controls carry it.
  • Zero radius and 1px grids fight macOS 27's rounded, glassy chrome; the popover will look foreign next to Control Center.

Platform fit

  • Easy to render identically everywhere; no blur, no materials.
  • Knobs need real drag physics to earn their place, which is real engineering.

Hairline

Direction 3 of 5

No accent colour at all. The headphones' own colourway is the only colour on screen, set on paper the shade of the Off White ULT WEAR, with Forest Gray for depth. Numbers are dot-matrix, rules are one pixel, and the whole app reads like a spec sheet you want to keep.

Where it shines

  • The most fashionable of the five; every row is scannable and the dot numerals are a signature.
  • Light and dark are trivial: swap paper for ink.

Risks

  • Strongly associated with Nothing. The off-white and forest tones are the defence, but it is a thin one.
  • "Cool" here is restraint, not spectacle. If you want the app to feel alive, this fights you.

Platform fit

  • Native-agnostic; renders identically anywhere.
  • The hairline grid maps cleanly onto a Windows tray flyout and a GNOME popover.

Glass

Direction 4 of 5

A real Mac app. Frosted sidebar and popover, solid content tiles, SF Pro, the system accent colour, segmented controls with a little depth. It looks like Apple shipped it next to the AirPods controls, which is exactly the comparison your users will make.

Where it shines

  • Fits your macOS-first priority perfectly; uses real system materials and gets macOS 27's look for free.
  • Lowest risk, fastest to ship with SwiftUI, and users already know how to use it.

Risks

  • Bento plus glass is the 2026 default; without a signature element it looks like any utility.
  • Least "cool" of the five. The ULT switch and a device render would have to carry the personality.

Platform fit

  • Mac: native. Windows: Mica window, Acrylic flyout, same structure. The two look different by design, which is the native trade-off.

Shell

Direction 5 of 5

The loudest option. A smoked translucent shell with chrome controls, a big ring dial for ambient level, glossy ULT buttons, a cyan light. It borrows from the translucent-electronics era the ULT line's bass-and-lights positioning already winks at. Memorable on sight, and the one most likely to date.

Where it shines

  • Nobody will mistake it for another headphone app. The ring dial is a genuine signature.
  • Chrome and glow sell the "powerful bass" story without a hero photo.

Risks

  • Dates fastest; glossy chrome reads as 2003 if executed carelessly.
  • Legibility on glass depends on what's behind it; needs solid reading zones and a reduced-transparency fallback.

Platform fit

  • Needs a web-tech or Metal renderer for the gradients and blur; Tauri on all three platforms is the natural stack.
  • Will clash with native Control Center on Mac, deliberately.

How to choose

If the goal is "feels like a Mac app, ships soonest", Glass. If the goal is "nobody has seen a headphone app like this", Stage or Shell. If you want the hardware feeling in your hands, Panel. If you want it to look expensive through restraint, Hairline.

Two directions mix well: Glass structure with Stage's light and number treatment gives a native Mac app with a real identity, and that is the combination I'd lean toward for a macOS-first, Windows-second product. But this is your call. Say which ones to keep, kill or merge, and I'll draw the next round, including the Sound and Scenes screens and the Windows flyout for whichever survives.
