Research report · desktop control of Sony Bluetooth headphones · ULT WEAR edition

Your ULT WEAR, the clients that support it, and what to build your own library on

You own a WH-ULT900N. There is no separate 2026 ULT WEAR model. It speaks Sony's v2 protocol, its ULT bass button is controllable over Bluetooth, and no major desktop client exposes it. Roughly sixty desktop projects exist, almost all from 2026, and four carry real weight. For a library that goes beyond Sony's app, start from mos9527's MIT-licensed libmdr and its extracted command tables.

Data pulled 2026-11-06 and 2026-11-07 Written for Roy, building a Sony headphone app and library Scope: Windows, macOS, Linux

Bottom line

Fork or vendor mos9527's libmdr (MIT, C++20) as the seed of your own library. It is the only codebase with machine-extracted command tables for all four Sony tables, pulled from Sony's own app binary, and it already defines the ULT mode structs your headphones use. Keep its code generator so new opcodes stay data-driven. If you want a Rust core, copy overheard's sans-IO crate layout and port the tables. Read Gadgetbridge and Resonance as references only, since their licenses (AGPL and PolyForm Noncommercial) would bind your library.

"Features Sony never gave" come from two places. First, opcodes the firmware answers but Sound Connect hides or mis-sends: codec readout, the device's real EQ layout, action logs, test and calibration modes, paired-device management over table 2, and settings a model accepts without declaring. Second, host-side logic the phone app brands as features: adaptive sound, scenes, per-app ambient, automatic multipoint switching, hotkeys, battery history. Firmware and voice-pack work is a separate Airoha transport with real brick risk; treat it as a later, optional module.

Your headphones: WH-ULT900N, not a 2026 model

No Sony press release, support page, review or project mentions a "ULT WEAR 2", "WH-ULT900N2" or "WH-ULT910N". The only ULT over-ear is the WH-ULT900N announced on 11 April 2024. Sony's 2026 headphone launches were the WF-1000XM6 and WH-1000XM6 colour refreshes, and the ULT WEAR went on clearance in April 2026. A unit bought in 2026 is a WH-ULT900N, most likely on firmware 2.1.0 (released 9 December 2025, security hardening only, no rollback). Earlier firmware was 2.0.5 (September 2024) and 2.0.7 (May 2025, iPhone ringtone fix). Firmware updates go through the phone app only.

Silicon
Sony "Integrated Processor V1" plus a MediaTek MT2822M Bluetooth SoC per the FCC-photo teardown. MediaTek's Airoha subsidiary sells the same silicon, which is why projects call it an "Airoha chipset" and why the device advertises the MTK firmware-transfer function.
Bluetooth
5.2, Classic only in practice. Codecs SBC, AAC, LDAC. LC3 was promised at launch but never appears in Sony's codec page or any firmware note, and the device advertises no LE Audio function. LDAC is unavailable while multipoint is on.
Protocol
Sony MDR v2 only, over RFCOMM. Service UUID 956C7B26-D49A-4BA8-B03F-B17D393CB6E2, channel 18 on firmware 2.1.0 (do not hard-code it). Protocol-info reply 01 00 03 00 20 02 00 00: version 0x03002002, command tables 1 and 2 enabled. The v1 UUID is not advertised. Only one control client at a time.
Identity
Bluetooth name "ULT WEAR" in all 2026 reports ("Sony ULT" in a December 2024 report, so match both). The BLE sibling "LE_ULT WEAR" is the ordinary GATT advertisement, not LE Audio. BlueZ modalias usb:v054Cp0F1Ed0210.
Features it has
ULT button (OFF / ULT1 / ULT2), noise cancelling, ambient sound with 20 levels and Focus on Voice, Quick Attention, 5-band EQ plus Clear Bass with presets, DSEE, multipoint, touch panel, wear detection with auto pause and auto power-off, 360 Reality Audio with head tracking, Quick Access (Spotify Tap, Endel, Amazon Music), voice assistant selection, voice guide volume, Adaptive Sound Control via the phone app.
Features it lacks
Speak-to-Chat and Find Your Headphones are not device features: neither appears in the help guide nor in the capability list, so a client should not render them for this model.

The ULT button on the wire

ULT mode is not a dedicated feature. It is multiplexed into the v2 equalizer family as inquired type 0x03 (PRESET_EQ_AND_ULT_MODE, support function 0x53). The six bands are Clear Bass plus five EQ bands (400 Hz, 1 kHz, 2.5 kHz, 6.3 kHz, 16 kHz), centred at 0x0A with 21 steps. Sony's own class name for the payload, THMSGV2T1EqParamUltMode, survives in libmdr's generated header.

GET   56 03
RET   57 03 <preset> <ult> 06 <b1..b6>      e.g. 57 03 00 02 06 0A 0A 0A 0A 0A 0A  (preset OFF, ULT2, flat)
SET   58 03 <preset> <ult> 06 <b1..b6>      ult: 00 OFF · 01 ULT1 · 02 ULT2
NTFY  59 03 ...                             (sent after any SET, even if the GET used type 00)
CAP   50 03  ->  51 03 06 15 0A 0C (<id> <name>)...   bands=6 steps=21 ult2step=0x0A presets=12

presets: 00 OFF · 10 BRIGHT ... 17 SPEECH · A0 CUSTOM · A1..A5 USER_SETTING1-5

Two hardware reports on firmware 2.1.0 disagree on one point: owaiskaifi found that type 03 ignores the preset and band bytes (a ULT-only setter, with type 00 for full EQ), while Z-ULT-Connect says presets must go through 58 03. Test both on your unit. A malformed EQ SET (band count without bands) corrupted the custom EQ slot and reset ULT in one report, so always read-modify-write.

Other byte contracts verified on this model: NC/ambient uses the 7-byte 68 17 layout (the older 8-byte layout puts the level in a byte the ULT WEAR ignores), and ambient fields are ignored while in NC mode. Battery is 22 00 with no threshold byte, which is what broke mos9527's percentage. Volume is A6/A8 20 on a 0 to 30 scale synced with AVRCP. Multipoint is d6 d1 to read, d8 d1 00 XX to set (inverted: 00 is on) followed by 98 00 06 01.

Building your own library: what to start from

Nothing is published on PyPI, crates.io, npm, pub.dev or NuGet as a Sony protocol library. Every reusable piece is an in-repo module of an app. Since you intend to write your own library and extend it past Sony's app, the question is not "which library do I link" but "which command tables, code generator and architecture do I inherit, under which license".

Seed from mos9527's libmdr Recommended

MIT, C++20. Payload structs are data-driven: each struct's first field is its opcode, trivially serializable payloads use a packed macro, dynamic ones get generated serialize, deserialize and validate code driven by // CODEGEN hints. Adding an opcode means adding an enum value and a struct, then regenerating. It has a stable C ABI, a transport-agnostic polled connection vtable, replay tests over captured fixtures in CI, and builds for every platform including Emscripten.

  • The tables were extracted from the Sound Connect iOS binary, where Java class names survive J2ObjC compilation. The checked-in JSON intermediate representation under tooling/ida/generated/ holds 623 payload declarations and 111 enums for v2 table 1 and 306 and 47 for table 2, and headers can be regenerated from it without IDA.
  • It already defines the ULT structs: EqUltModeStatus, EqParamUltMode and the RET, NTFY and capability variants.
  • Risks: a single core maintainer, pre-1.0 branch churn, and C++ FFI if your UI language is Rust, .NET or Swift. Vendoring a snapshot insulates you from the churn.

Rust core: copy overheard's shape Architecture reference

MIT or Apache-2.0, sans-IO workspace: mdr-frame (framing and stop-and-wait ARQ, no_std), mdr-proto (typed commands), mdr-session (handshake, ARQ, request-reply correlation, capability and state cache), an async client, and a transport crate for RFCOMM and GATT. Capability-gated rather than model-gated. Version 0.1.0, seven commits, Linux-only, verified on WF-C700N and WH-1000XM6 only. Port libmdr's tables into this layout rather than hand-typing them.

Device Center's sony-protocol Mid-size reference

MIT, C++. IProtocol, ProtocolV1/V2, FrameCodec, CapabilityDiscovery with a per-model cache, and a device profile registry that selects wire variants (10-band EQ, noise-control layout, detailed battery) from a profile. 111 hardware-free tests. Hand-coded and feature-oriented, not exhaustive. Its README prints service UUIDs that disagree with five other sources, so verify byte layouts before trusting them.

Read, never copy License-bound

  • Gadgetbridge (AGPL-3.0): the most battle-tested byte layouts and the broadest ULT WEAR feature set, but table 1 only, with many "not implemented for V2" stubs. Any derived library must be AGPL.
  • Resonance (PolyForm Noncommercial): the best prose protocol document, including table 2 group maps and tested opcodes, with evidence levels per row. Forbids commercial use.
  • ohm-app docs (CC0) and AndreasOlofsson/mdr-protocol: freely reusable but thin and dated.
  • Forking Plutoberth: archived, v1-only, write-only, cannot talk to your headphones. Not a foundation.

How commands get discovered

Five methods are in use. The highest-yield one in 2026 is static extraction from Sony's own binaries, which gives you the complete opcode and payload inventory at once. Dynamic capture then tells you which variant your specific model answers.

  • iOS binary via IDA. mos9527's tooling/ida pipeline loads the decrypted Sound Connect ARM64 binary, runs an extraction script that resolves J2ObjC descriptors and correlates serializers, and emits the JSON IR. The README calls itself an LLM-generated guide and requires you to source the app through an account and device you are authorized to use. Nothing from Sony's binary is committed, only derived IR.
  • Android APK via jadx. Resonance reconstructed its document from Sound Connect 13.3.0 this way. Enums keep their original names in package com.sony.songpal.tandemfamily.message.mdr, so values are not guesswork. Helper class names are obfuscated and only hold for that build.
  • HCI snoop plus Wireshark. Gadgetbridge's wiki has the generic steps: enable the Android HCI snoop log, drive Sony's app, pull the log, filter by MAC, bracket the unknown action with marker commands. Plutoberth's original contributors found this "extremely confusing" and that EQ changes left no trace, which is why static extraction won.
  • In-app frame recorders. mos9527 ships a Protocol Debugger that records, replays, modifies and sends packets, plus a scrubber that turns captures into test fixtures. Resonance has a CLI deepscan and a test mode that changes each setting and restores it. hush has a raw-frame command behind an allow-list. sony-ult-ctl has a live monitor for your exact model.
  • The capability handshake. Command 06 on both tables returns 07 00 <n> (<function> <priority>)×n. General settings are self-describing: their capability reply carries slot, type, format, name and summary strings such as SIDETONE_SETTING or TAP_SENSITIVITY_SETTING, so a library can enumerate many settings generically.

Features Sony never gave you

The firmware only answers commands it implements, so there is no secret developer unlock. What exists is larger than Sound Connect shows, in two layers.

Layer 1: on the wire, hidden or mis-sent by the app

  • Undeclared but answered opcodes. Resonance found the XM6 answers connection-mode 02 while declaring only E7, and accepts a Cinema-mode frame it does not declare. hush found the XM6 ambient sub-type that round-trips is 0x19, not the one Gadgetbridge sends. Nobody has catalogued this for the ULT WEAR, which is an opening for you.
  • The real EQ layout. The device self-describes its bands and steps (50 03 on your model, 5A 00 returns centre frequencies on 10-band models). Clients that hard-code Sony's UI layout miss this. On the ULT WEAR, combined EQ plus ULT control exists only in two tiny projects.
  • Codec readout. Opcodes 12/13 exist; Device Center shipped with the codec hard-coded to LDAC until someone actually queried it.
  • Action and operation logs. The device pushes C9 action logs unsolicited; the log inquired types include time-series operation logs and sound-dropout notifiers. Your ULT WEAR declares both notifiers. The app consumes these silently.
  • Test and calibration modes. GET_TEST 0x0F, NC test mode start and finish, face-tap test mode, call test sound, earbud-fit detection, head-gesture training, reset types. Sound Connect surfaces these only contextually.
  • Paired-device management over table 2. List remembered devices (36 02), connect, disconnect or forget (3C 02), switch audio source (3C 01), enter pairing mode (34 02). A full multipoint broker the desktop ecosystem mostly lacks.
  • Power inquired types beyond battery: caring charge, stamina, Bluetooth standby, battery safe mode. Which of these your model answers is untested.
  • Specific to your ULT WEAR, declared and unimplemented by every desktop client: Adaptive Sound Control (AUTO_NCASM 0x70), NC/AMB button cycle configuration, Quick Access app assignment, Safe Listening (table 2, 0x50), voice assistant selection and voice-guide volume, general settings 1 to 3 (touch panel, service link and more; payloads only in Gadgetbridge's Java), LDAC connection-mode switching (0xE3), DSEE auto-off (0xE2), factory reset. Gadgetbridge on Android has several of these, so the byte layouts are knowable.

Layer 2: host-side logic the phone app brands as features

Gadgetbridge's maintainer lists adaptive sound control, scene-based listening, auto-switch multipoint and Sound AR as "app-dependent": the phone detects activity or location and pushes an ordinary NC/ambient opcode. A desktop broker can do the same with the foreground app, time, location or audio focus as inputs. Nothing shipped does this yet.

  • CLI, global hotkeys and automation: the most-reacted open request on mos9527. hush and overheard ship CLIs that talk to a daemon over a socket.
  • Native OS battery: overheard feeds battery to BlueZ's BatteryProvider1 so the desktop shows it like any device, plus low-battery notifications. Battery history is a trivial extension.
  • A connection broker: the headphones take one control connection, so the GUI, daemon, hotkey tool and phone app all compete. Device Center lists an ownership broker as unpaid technical debt. Every mature client converged on a daemon for this reason.
  • Scenes and per-app ambient, automatic multipoint switching on audio focus, pairing-mode hold-off so auto-reconnect does not race the user.

Out of reach, or a different project

  • Firmware and voice packs are not Sony MDR at all. The MDR channel only toggles update mode; the transfer is Airoha RACE/FOTA over a second RFCOMM channel (UUID 8901dfa8-…) or BLE GATT. The container format is parsed, official images are mirrored, per-model AES keys have been recovered from flash dumps, custom voice packs are PC-installable on the XM4, and downgrades are device-verified. Nobody runs non-Sony-signed firmware because of the integrity gate. ERNW's 2025 disclosure (CVE-2025-20700 to 20702) lists the ULT WEAR among affected Airoha devices. This is feasible but model-specific, riskier, and a second transport.
  • Battery cycle count was not found as an opcode anywhere.
  • LDAC on Windows and macOS is a host-stack limit no control app can fix. Linux via PipeWire can switch codecs, which pats01 verified on the ULT WEAR.
  • Speak-to-Chat, Find Your Headphones: not features of this model.

Safety rules every mature project converged on

  • Gate every write behind the model's declared capability from the 06/07 handshake, and honour "blocked" status codes before a SET. mos9527 issue #76 documents an XM6 crash-and-reboot from setting a mode the device reported as blocked. Recoverable, but real.
  • Keep a hard deny-list for destructive opcodes, as hush does: F8 factory reset (on v2; on v1 the same byte is a harmless auto-power-off setting), 98 reboot, 24 power off, 2D firmware transfer, C4 JSON dump.
  • Read-modify-write EQ frames. One malformed EQ SET corrupted the custom slot on a ULT WEAR.
  • Treat any Airoha RACE flash write as high-risk and confine it to the device-reported FOTA partition. Stray writes are known to brick devices.

Protocol and platform facts that shape the design

No official SDK
Sony's support article says "There is no PC app available". The only public Sony API covers networked home audio over HTTP. Every project calls the headphone protocol unpublished.
One shared framer
Same wire format across generations, 8-bit sum checksum, stop-and-wait ARQ with one frame in flight. Every data frame must be acknowledged with the flipped sequence number or the device retransmits forever.
v1 vs v2
A different SDP UUID and a largely different opcode table, not a different protocol. Gadgetbridge detects the version from the init reply length. Opcode 0x22 means POWER_OFF in v1 and BATTERY in v2, which caused a real SonyBridge bug. Your ULT WEAR is v2 only.
Per-model quirks
Live inside v2, which is why runtime capability discovery beats static tables.
One connection
One control client at a time. The RFCOMM channel varies per model (18 on your unit, 9 and 15 elsewhere) and must come from SDP or a UUID connect.
0x3E | type | seq | len (u32 BE) | payload | checksum | 0x3C

escape:   0x3C, 0x3D, 0x3E in the body  ->  0x3D, (b & 0xEF)     (bites on e.g. EQ value 0x3E80)
checksum: 8-bit sum, excluding the 0x3E header
types:    ACK = 0x01   COMMAND_1 = 0x0C   COMMAND_2 = 0x0E
init:     COMMAND_1 "00 00"  ->  reply length 4 = v1, 8 = v2

v1 UUID:  96CC203E-5068-46ad-B32D-E316F5E069BA   (XM2/XM3/XM4, WF-1000XM3, XB900N and earlier)
v2 UUID:  956C7B26-D49A-4BA8-B03F-B17D393CB6E2   (WF-1000XM4 and WH-1000XM5 onward, ULT WEAR, CH720N, LinkBuds, WF-C)

Transports must be native

  • Windows: Winsock AF_BTH/BTHPROTO_RFCOMM with serviceClassId set, so the system resolves the channel with no SDP query and no manifest capability. WinRT's RfcommDeviceService only for packaged Store apps.
  • macOS: IOBluetooth RFCOMM. Not thread-safe, delegates on the main run loop, which pushes toward a Swift or ObjC transport under a C++ or Rust core. All surveyed Mac apps ship outside the App Store.
  • Linux: BlueZ RFCOMM sockets or the D-Bus Profile API; the bluer crate for Rust.
  • Generic wrappers (32feet.NET, Dart bluetooth_rfcomm, Node bluetooth-serial-port) are unproven on Sony devices and lack reliable SDP on Linux. Every surveyed client wrote its own three backends. Web Serial works as a transport in Chrome 89+ and Firefox 151+.

Legal posture

No project reports any action from Sony in more than five years and Plutoberth archived voluntarily. The shared stance: author from binaries you are authorized to use, publish derived facts and IR rather than Sony's code or assets, describe "bytes on the wire", disclaim affiliation, and keep "Sony" and "Sound Connect" out of the product name. No project has a lawyer-reviewed analysis of the Sound Connect terms or DMCA section 1201.

A concrete starting plan for the ULT WEAR

  1. Vendor libmdr and its generated IR. Take a snapshot of mos9527's libmdr, libmdr-bt and tooling/ida/generated/*.json under MIT. Decide now whether your core stays C++ with a C ABI or you port the tables into a Rust workspace shaped like overheard.
  2. Capture a baseline session from your own unit. Run mos9527's Protocol Debugger with recording on, or sony-ult-ctl's monitor, against your WH-ULT900N. Scrub it into a fixture. This gives you replay tests for a model mos9527 has no fixtures for.
  3. Build a conformance suite from published bytes. owaiskaifi's verified frames, sony-ult-ctl's NCASM and ULT frames, and SonyBridge's golden multipoint fixture cover handshake, battery, NC/ambient, ULT, volume and multipoint without a second device.
  4. Resolve the EQ-type dispute on your hardware. Test whether 58 03 honours the preset byte or only the ULT byte, and whether 58 00 is needed for full EQ. Check libmdr's EQ reply parser against 57 03 and fix it upstream if the third-party claim holds.
  5. Probe what the device answers beyond what it declares. Walk the generated opcode table with GET-only commands inside a deny-list, log replies, and compare against the 06/07 list. Start with general settings 1 to 3, Adaptive Sound Control, power inquired types, logs and the table 2 peripheral commands. This is the catalogue nobody has made.
  6. Design the daemon first. One process owns the RFCOMM channel and exposes a socket or D-Bus API; the GUI, CLI, hotkeys and automation are clients. This is what unlocks scenes, per-app ambient and auto-switching, and it is how you hand the channel back to the phone app cleanly.
  7. Ship what nobody ships. Signed and notarized binaries, a store listing, and a CLI. BudsLink's Flathub numbers show the demand a listing unlocks.
  8. Leave Airoha RACE for a separate module. Only after the MDR library is solid, and only with the FOTA-partition restriction the voice-pack projects enforce.
